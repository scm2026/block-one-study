Block One

Five cases in the order the index put them, built to show the buildup rather than the cases. One difficulty dial turns at a time where the corpus allows it; where it doesn't, the jump is marked and a bridge is written in. The toolkit, just above each case, fills as you go.

Page tint

Every step is structured the same way: what you have, what to ask for and why — tagged clarify, unlock or test — what you still won't have, then the chain of arithmetic or the chain of reasoning. Source and notes are split into labelled rows rather than prose. "What you have" is only ever what the case hands over unprompted — an unlock question that pays off shows what it reveals directly underneath it, so a fact you'd have to ask for never looks like one you were just given.

What each case turns up — and what it leaves alone

The same nine boxes sit in the same places in every case. What changes is which ones the case lights up — and two of these five never fill in a single number.

Every prompt, figure, question and model answer belongs to Booth 2025 and is page-anchored to it. The canvas, the sequence, the dials, the ask-lists and the bridges are ours. Where the casebook's own arithmetic or logic doesn't hold, its answer is left standing and the objection sits beside it.
